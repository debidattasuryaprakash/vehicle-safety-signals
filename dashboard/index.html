Does the signal rule work?

Backtest, all makes, on months with a full year of follow-up: the share of flagged months followed by a recall on the same make, model and component within 365 days, by z-score threshold. ≥ 1.5× baseline ≥ 2× ≥ 3× every eligible month (base rate)

Signals

Months where complaints reached at least 2× their 12-month baseline, with at least 10 complaints and a Poisson z-score of 3 or more. Counted per make, model and component. Spikes within 90 days after a recall on the same model and component are left out, since owners are reacting to the recall. Select a row to see its history.

Recalls and the complaints before them

Complaints for the same make, model and component in the 180 days before each recall report. Select a row for the defect, its consequence and the remedy, straight from NHTSA.

Method. Python loads NHTSA's flat files into Snowflake (or DuckDB locally); dbt models clean them, build a star schema and compute a trailing 12-month baseline per make, model and component. Vehicles only (no tires, child seats or equipment); the catch-all “Unknown or other” component is never flagged. Thresholds live in dbt_project.yml. A signal is a prompt to investigate, not proof of a defect. Model lineage and column docs (dbt).
